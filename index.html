A study of constrained reinforcement learning for whole-body hazard avoidance by a humanoid robot standing on a limited support surface.

01 · Abstract

Abstract

Humanoid robots operating in unstructured environments must remain balanced while reacting to fast-moving hazards. We study whole-body evasion on a constrained base: the robot stands on a small platform and must dodge flying hazards through whole-body motion while remaining upright. We formulate the task as a constrained Markov decision process and introduce a modified CRAX environment with explicit fall and bullet-contact costs. We train policies with constrained reinforcement learning on a three-level curriculum: one slow bullet, three medium-speed bullets, and six fast bullets. We then compare curriculum training with direct training and study how changing the hazard from small bullets to large planes changes the learned behaviour and safety.

02 · Research questions

Research Questions

The study evaluates whether curriculum learning provides an advantage over direct training and whether the geometry and scale of the hazard influence the resulting policy and safety performance.

RQ1

Does the curriculum improve over training directly on the final level?

We compare a Curriculum agent trained through levels 1 → 2 → 3 with a Direct agent trained only on the final six-fast-bullet level, using the same constrained algorithm and total training budget.

RQ2

How does the hazard type change what the agent learns and how safe it is?

We compare small bullets with large planes. Bullets can often be avoided by moving one body part or leaning; planes cover more of the body and can require crouching or whole-body motion.

04 · Method

Methodology

The experimental environment extends the CRAX humanoid with modifications intended to support stable standing, explicit body contacts, and constrained whole-body evasion.

Modified CRAX humanoid

Flat 26 × 10 cm feet, two actuated ankle joints per foot, strengthened motors, colliding body parts and a stiffer contact solver. The robot has 21 actuators instead of 17.

Constrained RL

PPO-Lag optimizes reward while constraining expected safety cost. Bullet-contact and fall costs are combined under a budget of 0.1 per episode in the bullet task.

Whole-body observations

The policy receives 328 observation values, including torso state, joint angles and velocities, body inertial quantities, actuator forces, platform position and relative bullet information.

Two hazard families

The bullet task uses small spherical projectiles. The plane task uses a large tilted square plane, requiring qualitatively different avoidance behaviour.

05 · Curriculum

Curriculum Learning

The curriculum progressively increases hazard difficulty. At each stage, the learned policy, value networks, and observation normalizer are transferred to the next level.

LEVEL 01

Single bullet

The agent first learns to maintain balance and remain on the constrained base while responding to a single, relatively slow projectile. This stage establishes the basic relationship between whole-body motion, hazard avoidance, and postural stability.

1 bullet · 0.75-1.5 m/s
LEVEL 02

Several bullets

The second stage introduces three medium-speed projectiles with tighter lateral and vertical spread. The policy must coordinate whole-body motion across multiple incoming hazards while continuing to avoid falls and unnecessary contacts.

3 bullets · 1.5-3.0 m/s
LEVEL 03

Fast evasion

The final bullet level presents six fast projectiles with the highest speeds and the tightest spread. The agent must react quickly, select effective evasive motions, and recover its posture while remaining within the limited support area.

6 bullets · 3.0-5.0 m/s

Key finding

Curriculum learning helped reduce bullet contact, but it did not improve falls. For the plane task, the intermediate curriculum level taught a strategy of standing tall and accepting the hazard - making the curriculum dramatically worse than direct training.
